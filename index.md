## How to drill

1. **Cover the right column**, read the left, say the answer out loud.
2. Do one table per sitting. Repeat yesterday's table first (2 minutes).
3. When a table is 100% cold-recall twice in a row, move it to the "monthly" pile.
4. Verbs: always drill **all six persons in one breath** — γράφω-γράφεις-γράφει-γράφουμε-γράφετε-γράφουν.

## The four cases — what they actually do

Greek marks a noun's **job in the sentence** by changing its ending *and* its article.
English does this with word order ("the dog bites the man"); Greek does it with endings,
which is why Greek word order can move around freely without changing the meaning.

Full endings for every noun type are on page 4. This section is only about **what each case is for**.

### Quick reference

| Case | Greek name | What it marks | Answers | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Nominative** | Ονομαστική | the **subject** — who or what does it | Ποιος; Τι; | **Ο Γιάννης** διαβάζει. — **John** is reading. |
| **Accusative** | Αιτιατική | the **direct object**, and anything after a preposition | Ποιον; Τι; | Βλέπω **τον Γιάννη**. — I see **John**. |
| **Genitive** | Γενική | **possession**, and the person something is given to | Τίνος; | Το σπίτι **του Γιάννη** — **John's** house |
| **Vocative** | Κλητική | **calling** someone | — | **Γιάννη**, έλα! — **John**, come! |

### The same name in all four

| Case | Form | In a sentence | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | ο Γιάνν**ης** | **Ο Γιάννης** ήρθε. | John came. |
| Accusative | τον Γιάνν**η** | Είδα **τον Γιάννη**. | I saw John. |
| Genitive | του Γιάνν**η** | Το αυτοκίνητο **του Γιάννη**. | John's car. |
| Vocative | Γιάνν**η**! | **Γιάννη**, πού είσαι; | John, where are you? |

The same thing with a feminine noun:

| Case | Form | In a sentence | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | η δασκάλ**α** | **Η δασκάλα** μιλάει. | The teacher is speaking. |
| Accusative | τη δασκάλ**α** | Ρωτάω **τη δασκάλα**. | I ask the teacher. |
| Genitive | της δασκάλ**ας** | Το βιβλίο **της δασκάλας**. | The teacher's book. |
| Vocative | δασκάλ**α**! | **Δασκάλα**, μια ερώτηση! | Teacher, a question! |

### 1 · Nominative — the doer

| Use | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| Subject of the verb | **Ο σκύλος** τρώει. | The dog is eating. |
| Subject (plural) | **Τα παιδιά** παίζουν. | The children are playing. |
| After **είμαι** ⚠ | Ο πατέρας μου είναι **γιατρός**. | My father is a doctor. |
| After **γίνομαι** ⚠ | Έγινε **δάσκαλος**. | He became a teacher. |

⚠ This is the form dictionaries list. ⚠ After είμαι / γίνομαι the word **stays nominative** —
both sides of the sentence are subjects, so nothing changes: Αυτός είναι **ο Γιάννης**.

### 2 · Accusative — the receiver, and after every preposition

| Use | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| Direct object | Αγόρασα **ένα βιβλίο**. | I bought a book. |
| Direct object | Περιμένω **τον αδερφό μου**. | I'm waiting for my brother. |
| After **σε** | Πάω **στο σχολείο**. | I go to school. |
| After **από** | Ήρθα **από τη δουλειά**. | I came from work. |
| After **με** | Μιλάω **με τη Μαρία**. | I'm talking to Maria. |
| After **για** | Είναι **για σένα**. | It's for you. |
| How long | Έμεινα **τρεις μέρες**. | I stayed three days. |

⚠ **Every** common preposition takes the accusative — σε, από, με, για, χωρίς, μέχρι, προς,
μετά, πριν, κατά. There is nothing to choose: after a preposition, accusative.

⚠ Some Greek verbs take a plain accusative where English needs a preposition:

| Greek | English |
|---|---|
| περιμένω **τον Γιάννη** | wait **for** John |
| ψάχνω **τα κλειδιά μου** | look **for** my keys |
| κοιτάζω **τη θάλασσα** | look **at** the sea |
| πληρώνω **τον λογαριασμό** | pay **for** the bill |

### 3 · Genitive — "of", "'s", and "to someone"

| Use | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| Possession | Το σπίτι **του Γιάννη** | John's house |
| Possession | Η πόρτα **του σπιτιού** | the door of the house |
| Possession (plural) | Η μητέρα **των παιδιών** | the children's mother |
| Indirect object | Δίνω **του Γιάννη** ένα δώρο. | I give John a present. |
| Possessive pronoun | το σπίτι **μου** | my house |

⚠ Greek has **no dative case**. "To / for someone" is either the genitive **or** σε + accusative,
and in everyday speech **σε + accusative wins**:

| Genitive | σε + accusative (more common) |
|---|---|
| Δίνω **της Μαρίας** το βιβλίο. | Δίνω το βιβλίο **στη Μαρία**. |
| Λέω **του παιδιού** την αλήθεια. | Λέω την αλήθεια **στο παιδί**. |

⚠ But with **weak pronouns** the genitive is the normal choice: **Μου** έδωσε το βιβλίο (he gave
**me** the book). Those little words — μου, σου, του, της, μας, σας, τους — *are* the genitive.

⚠ Word order: the genitive comes **after** the thing owned — το σπίτι του Γιάννη, never the reverse.

### 4 · Vocative — calling someone

Used only when you speak **to** a person directly. It never takes an article.

| Nominative | Vocative | Note |
|---|---|---|
| ο Γιάννης | **Γιάννη!** | -ης → -η |
| ο Πέτρος | **Πέτρο!** | names in -ος → -ο |
| ο κύριος | **Κύριε!** | common nouns in -ος → -ε |
| ο φίλος | **Φίλε!** | |
| ο γιατρός | **Γιατρέ!** | |
| η Μαρία | **Μαρία!** | feminine unchanged |
| το παιδί | **Παιδί!** | neuter unchanged |
| τα παιδιά | **Παιδιά!** | very common |

⚠ Never put the article in front: **Γιάννη**, έλα! — not ~~Ο Γιάννη, έλα!~~

### Why the endings matter: word order can move

| Greek | English | |
|---|---|---|
| **Ο Γιάννης** βλέπει **τη Μαρία**. | John sees Maria. | neutral order |
| **Τη Μαρία** βλέπει **ο Γιάννης**. | John sees **Maria**. | same meaning, Maria emphasised |

**ο Γιάννης** is nominative and **τη Μαρία** accusative, so you always know who is doing the
seeing — no matter which one comes first. In English, swapping the two would reverse the meaning.

### Which case do I need?

| Ask yourself | Case |
|---|---|
| Is it doing the action? | **Nominative** |
| Does it come after είμαι / γίνομαι? | **Nominative** |
| Is it receiving the action directly? | **Accusative** |
| Is there a preposition in front of it? | **Accusative**, always |
| Does it mean "of…" or "…'s"? | **Genitive** |
| Is it the person given / told something? | **Genitive**, or σε + accusative |
| Am I calling this person? | **Vocative** |

### Drill

| Which case, and why? | Answer |
|---|---|
| Βλέπω **τον γιατρό**. | accusative — direct object |
| **Ο γιατρός** ήρθε. | nominative — subject |
| Το γραφείο **του γιατρού**. | genitive — possession |
| **Γιατρέ**, πονάω! | vocative — calling him |
| Πάω **στον γιατρό**. | accusative — after σε |
| Η Άννα είναι **δασκάλα**. | nominative — after είμαι |
| Έδωσα **της Άννας** το βιβλίο. | genitive — indirect object |
| Μείναμε **δύο μέρες**. | accusative — how long |

In the HTML and PDF the gender bands are real cell backgrounds that fill the whole row.

### Theme switch

Every HTML file (the combined one and each per-sheet file) has a pill button in the top-right
corner that cycles:

| Label | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| **Auto** | follows the OS / browser setting via `prefers-color-scheme` (the default) |
| **Light** | pinned light, ignores the OS setting |
| **Dark** | pinned dark, ignores the OS setting |

The choice is stored in `localStorage` under `greek-a2-theme` and survives reloads. A tiny
script in `<head>` applies it before first paint, so a pinned dark theme does not flash white.
Dark mode uses deeper gender bands with light text rather than the pastel ones.

#### Why `color-scheme` is written the way it is

Do not "simplify" these three declarations:

| Selector | Value | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| `:root` | `light dark` | must advertise dark support **at all times** |
| `:root[data-theme="light"]` | `only light` | explicit opt-out of forced darkening |
| `:root[data-theme="dark"]` | `only dark` | same, for the pinned dark theme |

Chrome's **Auto Dark Mode for Web Contents** (`chrome://flags/#enable-force-dark`) inverts any
page whose root resolves to light-only. With plain `color-scheme: light` the Light setting was
silently inverted back to a black background, so the toggle appeared to do nothing but nudge the
text colour. The `only` keyword is the documented opt-out. Verified by rendering each state with
`--enable-features=WebContentsForceDark` and confirming the output is byte-identical to a normal
render.

⚠ The **PDF is always light**, whatever theme is selected — the print stylesheet resets the
palette. Worth knowing because headless Chrome reports `prefers-color-scheme: dark`, so without
that reset the PDF would have printed dark.
