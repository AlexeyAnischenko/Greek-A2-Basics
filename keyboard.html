How the accents work
KeyWhat it doesExample
Hover Point at any letter and a balloon shows its name and pronunciation — ο is "omicron", pronounced like the o in not. Several keys add a note about how they change in context (γ before ε/ι, υ in αυ/ευ, σ before μ β γ δ). ο → omicron
Long press Hold a vowel to get its accented form, the way a phone keyboard works. Keys with something to offer have a small dot in the corner. ι and υ have three forms each, so holding them opens a chooser — tap one, or slide onto it and let go. Holding σ gives final ς. hold α → ά
΄ tonos Accents the vowel just before the cursor. If the character before the cursor is not an accentable vowel, it arms instead and the next vowel you type gets the accent. α → ά
¨ dialytika Same behaviour, for ι and υ. ι → ϊ
΅ both Dialytika + tonos together. ι → ΐ
⇧ Shift One capital letter. Click twice to lock (caps). α → Α
ς Final sigma — only at the end of a word (μαθητής), σ everywhere else.

The armed state is shown by the key turning orange. Press it again to cancel.

Typing on your real keyboard

Latin keys follow the standard Greek layout, so a→α, s→σ, d→δ, w→ς, q→; (Greek question mark) and ;→΄ (tonos). Shift gives capitals. Ctrl+Enter copies.


Turn this off if you already have a Greek layout installed in Windows — otherwise the two would fight each other.
