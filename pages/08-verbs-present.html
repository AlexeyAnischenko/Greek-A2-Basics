08 · Present Tense (Ενεστώτας)

Greek verbs are cited by their 1st person singular: γράφω = "I write". The present covers both English "I write" and "I am writing".

Three conjugation groups — how to tell them apart

Group1st singular ends inStressExample
A-ωon the stemγράφω
B1-άω / -ώon the endingαγαπάω / αγαπώ
B2-ώ (never -άω)on the endingμπορώ

Master grid — every ending, every tense ⚠ the overview for pages 10–13

Same shape as the noun grid on page 4 and the adjective grid on page 7: one row per tense and group, the three persons across the top, each split into singular and plural. The cell holds the ending — the stem that goes in front of it is named in the row label, and θα / να / έχω are printed in the cell because they are separate words you have to say. Hover a cell (or tap it) for a sentence that uses exactly that form.

Rows are banded and grouped by tense family: present amber, past rose, future and subjunctive cyan. The tenses themselves are taught on page 11 (past), page 12 (future & να) and page 13 (perfect) — this grid is for reciting, not for meeting them the first time.

είμαι and έχω — the two with no dependent stem

What the grid shows at a glance:

PatternWhere it holds
The whole rose band is one set of endings: -α, -ες, -ε, -αμε, -ατε, -ανaorist and imperfect, all three groups — only the stem in the label changes
The whole cyan band is one set of endings: -ω, -εις, -ει, -ουμε, -ετε, -ουνθα and να take present-tense endings, on either stem
So there are really two ending sets to learn, not sixplus the passive ones, and everything else is stems
θα + dependent vs θα + presentthe only difference is aspect: once (θα γράψω) vs repeatedly (θα γράφω)
Never an έ- augment after θα or ναθα γράψω, not θα έγραψα — the augment belongs to the past alone
The 1st and 2nd plural lose the έ-γράψαμε, γράψατε — three syllables already carry the stress
The perfect conjugates only έχωthe second word is frozen: έχω / έχεις / έχει γράψει
Passive 1st plural is -όμαστε now, -όμασταν in the pastthe one ending you will reach for wrongly
Passive aorist shifts the stress in the pluralπλύθηκα but πλυθήκαμε ⚠
είμαι / έχω have no aorist and no dependentone past each (ήμουν, είχα), one future each (θα είμαι, θα έχω)

⚠ The passive imperfect (πλενόμουν, ερχόμουν) is the one paradigm on this grid that has no table of its own on pages 11–13. It is here because ερχόμουν, καθόμουν and κοιμόμουν come up constantly when you tell a story in the past.
