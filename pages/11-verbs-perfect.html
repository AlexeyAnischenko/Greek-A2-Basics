2. The perfect: έχω + invariable form

PronounPersonForm

Only έχω conjugates. The second word is frozen.

3. When to use it — and when NOT to ⚠

Greek uses the aorist far more than English uses the simple past. The perfect is for experience and for results that matter now.

SituationGreek
Experience ("ever")Έχεις πάει ποτέ στην Κρήτη; — Have you ever been to Crete?
Result nowΈχω χάσει τα κλειδιά μου. — I've lost my keys.
Not yetΔεν έχω φάει ακόμα. — I haven't eaten yet.
AlreadyΈχω ήδη διαβάσει αυτό το βιβλίο.

The two traps

English⚠ Wrong in Greek✔ Right in Greek
I have lived here five yearsΈχω μείνει εδώ πέντε χρόνιαΜένω εδώ πέντε χρόνια. (PRESENT)
Yesterday I went to the cinemaΧθες έχω πάειΧθες πήγα στο σινεμά. (AORIST)

Rule of thumb: with a stated past time (χθες, πέρσι, στις 5) always use the aorist. With "for / since" up to now, use the present.
