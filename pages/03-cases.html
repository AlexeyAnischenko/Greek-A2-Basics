03 · The four cases (Πτώσεις) — what they actually do

Greek marks a noun's job in the sentence by changing its ending and its article. English does this with word order ("the dog bites the man"); Greek does it with endings, which is why Greek word order can move around freely without changing the meaning.

The endings themselves are on page 4. This sheet is about what each case is for — what those four column headings actually mean, and which question word asks for which one.

1 · Nominative — the doer

UseExampleEnglish
Subject of the verbΟ σκύλος τρώει.The dog is eating.
Subject (plural)Τα παιδιά παίζουν.The children are playing.
After είμαι ⚠Ο πατέρας μου είναι γιατρός.My father is a doctor.
After γίνομαι ⚠Έγινε δάσκαλος.He became a teacher.

⚠ This is the form dictionaries list. ⚠ After είμαι / γίνομαι the word stays nominative — both sides of the sentence are subjects, so nothing changes: Αυτός είναι ο Γιάννης.

2 · Accusative — the receiver, and after every preposition

UseExampleEnglish
Direct objectΑγόρασα ένα βιβλίο.I bought a book.
Direct objectΠεριμένω τον αδερφό μου.I'm waiting for my brother.
After σεΠάω στο σχολείο.I go to school.
After απόΉρθα από τη δουλειά.I came from work.
After μεΜιλάω με τη Μαρία.I'm talking to Maria.
After γιαΕίναι για σένα.It's for you.
How longΈμεινα τρεις μέρες.I stayed three days.

⚠ Every common preposition takes the accusative — σε, από, με, για, χωρίς, μέχρι, προς, μετά, πριν, κατά. There is nothing to choose: after a preposition, accusative.

⚠ Some Greek verbs take a plain accusative where English needs a preposition:

3 · Genitive — "of", "'s", and "to someone"

UseExampleEnglish
PossessionΤο σπίτι του ΓιάννηJohn's house
PossessionΗ πόρτα του σπιτιούthe door of the house
Possession (plural)Η μητέρα των παιδιώνthe children's mother
Indirect objectΔίνω του Γιάννη ένα δώρο.I give John a present.
Possessive pronounτο σπίτι μουmy house

⚠ Greek has no dative case. "To / for someone" is either the genitive or σε + accusative, and in everyday speech σε + accusative wins:

Genitiveσε + accusative (more common)
Δίνω της Μαρίας το βιβλίο.Δίνω το βιβλίο στη Μαρία.
Λέω του παιδιού την αλήθεια.Λέω την αλήθεια στο παιδί.

⚠ But with weak pronouns the genitive is the normal choice: Μου έδωσε το βιβλίο (he gave me the book). Those little words — μου, σου, του, της, μας, σας, τους — are the genitive.

⚠ Word order: the genitive comes after the thing owned — το σπίτι του Γιάννη, never the reverse.

5 · Which question word asks for which case

The useful trick: the answer comes back in the same case as the question word. Ask with a nominative and you get a nominative back — so the question tells you which ending to use.

⚠ Τι never changes shape, but its job does. Compare Τι έγινε; (subject — nominative) with Τι θέλεις; (object — accusative). Only the role differs, not the word.

⚠ There is no vocative question word. You cannot ask someone into being called — the vocative is only used when you address them directly.

6 · The question words that are not about case

These ask about circumstances rather than about a noun's job, so no case is involved in the question. Their answers usually still use a preposition + accusative.

⚠ In questions these carry a written accent: πού, πότε, πώς. The same words without the accent are conjunctions and relatives — που, πως (page 18).

Why the endings matter: word order can move

GreekEnglish
Ο Γιάννης βλέπει τη Μαρία.John sees Maria.neutral order
Τη Μαρία βλέπει ο Γιάννης.John sees Maria.same meaning, Maria emphasised

ο Γιάννης is nominative and τη Μαρία accusative, so you always know who is doing the seeing — no matter which one comes first. In English, swapping the two would reverse the meaning.

Which case do I need?

Ask yourselfCase
Is it doing the action?Nominative
Does it come after είμαι / γίνομαι?Nominative
Is it receiving the action directly?Accusative
Is there a preposition in front of it?Accusative, always
Does it mean "of…" or "…'s"?Genitive
Is it the person given / told something?Genitive, or σε + accusative
Am I calling this person?Vocative
